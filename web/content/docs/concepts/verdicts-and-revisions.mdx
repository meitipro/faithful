---
title: The three verdicts and revisions
description: What FAITHFUL, FLAWED and WRONG LANGUAGE do to the money and the claim.
---

A submission that passes the exact checks waits for `judge`. Validators each ask their own model the
[judge prompt](/docs/more/judge-prompt) and agree on one label. Only the label crosses consensus; the reason sentence
is the leader's, stored for the translator, and never compared.

## FAITHFUL

Same meaning, nothing added or left out, every warning, condition and number preserved, glossary followed, and
natural enough that a native reader of technical docs would not stumble.

- The reserved rate moves from the pool to the translator's balance.
- The section is approved in that language and joins the export.

## FLAWED

Any change of meaning, omission, addition, softened or reversed warning, wrong glossary term, or wording so unnatural
it confuses. The judge names the first problem it found.

- The first FLAWED verdict keeps the claim with the translator for **one revision**, within the same 48 hours.
- A second FLAWED verdict releases the claim. The reserve returns to the pool and the section reopens for everyone
  else.

## WRONG LANGUAGE

The text is not in the target language, or is not a translation of the source. The claim is released at once.

## Somewhere to go, and no asking again until it suits

A translator released from a section by a verdict may not claim that same section in that language again: otherwise
the same text could be resubmitted until a committee said yes. They can claim every other section, and the released
one is open to every other translator. A claim that simply expired carries no such bar.

## When validators do not agree

If the committee cannot agree on one label, the transaction ends without a verdict and nothing changes: the
submission is still waiting, and anyone can call `judge` again for a new round. A model answer that cannot be read is
retried once and then fails the round; it never becomes a verdict.
